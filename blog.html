Two Internships, Two Kinds of Data

This post is about two very different problems I worked on during my internship—and how they changed my understanding of what business analytics really is.

When I first started learning analytics, I thought it meant working with spreadsheets, SQL queries, dashboards, and machine learning models. My internship showed me that analytics is much broader than that. Sometimes the challenge is finding insights from thousands of historical records. Other times, it's making business decisions when almost no data exists.

Learning from Historical Data – JustBooks Solutions

At JustBooks Solutions, I analyzed 10,645 member records collected over 15 years across 47 branches. The objective was to identify inactive members who should be prioritized for membership renewal campaigns.

The project involved customer segmentation, identifying meaningful variables, and discovering why one branch contributed nearly 20% of all inactive members.

Building Analytics Without Data – PESU Venture Labs

During my internship at PESU Venture Labs, I worked with Kreedo and BAE Social. Since both startups were in their early stages, there was almost no historical customer data available.

Instead of relying on dashboards or reports, I performed market research, competitor analysis, customer segmentation, pricing analysis, and business strategy evaluation to support decision-making.

Key Learnings

  • Analytics is not only about data.
  • Historical data helps validate decisions.
  • Market research helps when historical data doesn't exist.
  • The best analytical approach depends on the business problem.

Final Thoughts

Working on both projects taught me that business analytics is ultimately about reducing uncertainty and helping organizations make informed decisions using the best evidence available.
